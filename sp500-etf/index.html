VOO vs. SPY vs. IVV: What's the Difference?

They track the same index. VOO and IVV cost 0.03%; SPY costs 0.0945% but has the deepest options market and heaviest trading, so active traders prefer it.

  • SPY is a unit investment trust, so it cannot lend securities or reinvest dividends before paying them out.
  • SPYM offers the same index for 0.02% at a lower share price.

S&P 500 ETF Outside the US

UK and EU retail investors usually can't buy a US-listed S&P 500 ETF like VOO or SPY because it has no PRIIPs Key Information Document. They use UCITS versions such as CSPX or VUAA instead, while Australians often use the ASX-listed IVV.

S&P 500 ETF FAQ

Which S&P 500 ETF is best?

For long-term investors, the cheapest: SPYM (0.02%), VOO or IVV (0.03%). For active trading and options, SPY is the most liquid.

Is VOO or SPY better?

For buy-and-hold investors, VOO — it costs 0.03% a year versus SPY's 0.0945%. For frequent traders and options users, SPY's liquidity often matters more than the fee.

How much money do I need to buy an S&P 500 ETF?

With fractional shares, as little as a few dollars. There is no fund minimum when you buy through a brokerage account.

Do S&P 500 ETFs pay dividends?

Yes, every quarter. VOO's 30-day SEC yield was 1.00% on Sep 30, 2026.

Can an S&P 500 ETF lose money?

Yes, during broader market downturns. The S&P 500 experiences cyclical pullbacks, but historically it has averaged about 10.5% a year over multi-decade periods, recovering to new highs.

Can I buy VOO or SPY in the UK?

Usually not as a retail investor — US-domiciled ETFs lack the Key Information Document UK and EU rules require. UCITS versions such as CSPX, VUAA or SPY5 track the same index.
