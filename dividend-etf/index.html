Dividend ETF

A dividend ETF holds companies that pay regular cash dividends and passes that income to you, every quarter or month. Popular funds yield about 1.5% to 3.4%; option-income funds such as JEPI pay more, with trade-offs.

Are JEPI and JEPQ Real Dividend ETFs?

Not exactly. They sell options to generate income, which produces high monthly payouts but limits gains in strong markets. Much of that income is taxed as ordinary income rather than qualified dividends.

  • Higher payout does not mean higher total return.
  • For steady, tax-efficient income, traditional dividend ETFs like SCHD or VYM are simpler.

How Dividend ETF Income Is Taxed

Qualified dividends are taxed at long-term rates of 0%, 15% or 20%; other distributions at ordinary income rates. Your broker reports both on Form 1099-DIV.

  • Holding dividend ETFs in an IRA or Roth IRA avoids yearly taxes on payouts.
  • Looking for safer income? Compare Treasury ETF yields.

Dividend ETF Outside the US

UK and EU income investors usually choose a UCITS dividend ETF such as Vanguard VHYL or SPDR USDV, because US-listed dividend ETFs lack a PRIIPs Key Information Document. Australians often use Vanguard VHY, whose payouts can carry franking credits.

Dividend ETF FAQ

What is the best dividend ETF?

It depends on your goal. SCHD (0.06%, 3.37% yield) is popular for income and quality; VYM (0.04%) for broad high yield; VIG and DGRO for growing dividends.

How much do I need to invest to make $1,000 a month in dividends?

About $356,000 at SCHD's 3.37% yield, or about $511,000 at VYM's 2.35%. Use the calculator above with any yield.

Which dividend ETFs pay monthly?

JEPI and JEPQ pay monthly. SCHD, VYM, VIG and DGRO pay quarterly.

Is SCHD a good investment?

SCHD is a benchmark dividend ETF with a low 0.06% fee and a 3.37% yield, screening for fundamentally strong dividend-paying US companies with consistent dividend track records.

Are ETF dividends guaranteed?

ETF distributions reflect actual cash dividends paid by underlying holdings. Payouts can fluctuate quarterly based on corporate earnings and board dividend declarations.

Do franking credits apply to a global dividend ETF?

No. Franking credits only come with dividends from companies that paid Australian tax, so they apply to funds like VHY but not to global funds such as VHYL or USDV.
