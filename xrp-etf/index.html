XRP ETF or Real XRP?

An ETF fits an IRA and a brokerage account. Owning XRP directly lets you trade 24/7, move it to your own wallet and avoid a yearly fee.

  • Spot crypto ETFs are taxed like the coin itself — as property, at capital gains rates.
  • Staking rewards from Solana ETFs may be taxed as ordinary income.
  • Compare with the much larger bitcoin ETF market.

XRP ETF Outside the US

Outside the US, XRP ETF exposure comes from physically backed European XRP ETPs such as WisdomTree XRPW or 21Shares AXRP. Australia has no locally listed XRP ETF yet, and PRIIPs rules keep US-listed XRP ETFs off UK and EU retail platforms.

XRP ETF FAQ

Is there an XRP ETF?

Yes. Five US spot XRP ETFs trade as of October 2026: XRPZ, XRPC, XRP, TOXR and GXRP.

Which XRP ETF has the lowest fee?

Franklin's XRPZ at 0.19% a year. Canary's XRPC is the most expensive at 0.50%.

Do XRP ETFs pay staking rewards?

No. None of the US spot XRP ETFs stake their XRP. Solana ETFs, by contrast, do stake and pass rewards through.

Is there a Solana ETF?

Yes. Several US spot Solana ETFs trade, including BSOL, FSOL, GSOL, VSOL and MSOL. BSOL is the largest, with about $1.2 billion.

Can I buy an XRP ETF in a Roth IRA?

Yes, at brokers that offer these funds. Gains inside a Roth IRA can be tax-free if you follow the withdrawal rules.

Can Europeans buy US XRP ETFs?

Generally no. US spot XRP ETFs such as Franklin XRPZ lack the Key Information Document EU and UK rules require, so retail brokers don't offer them. European investors use locally listed XRP ETPs like WisdomTree XRPW instead.
