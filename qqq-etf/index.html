QQQ or QQQM: Which Should You Buy?

For long-term investing, QQQM — same holdings for 0.15% instead of 0.18%. For active trading and options, QQQ, which has far more trading volume.

  • Both pay a small quarterly dividend (about 0.4% 30-day SEC yield).
  • Leverage multiplies risk — see how decay works in our leveraged ETF guide.

QQQ ETF Returns

The Nasdaq-100 returned 21.16% a year over 10 years and 16.56% a year over 5 years through September 30, 2026 — versus 15.33% a year over 10 years for the S&P 500.

  • Higher returns came with higher risk: QQQ fell about 33% in 2022.
  • Tech makes up most of the index, so one sector drives results.
  • For broader exposure, compare our S&P 500 ETF guide.

QQQ ETF Outside the US

The US-listed QQQ ETF isn't sold to UK and EU retail investors because it has no PRIIPs Key Information Document. They use Invesco EQQQ or iShares CNDX, which track the same Nasdaq-100; Australians typically use Betashares NDQ.

QQQ ETF FAQ

Is QQQ a good investment?

It has been one of the best-performing large ETFs, returning 21.16% a year over 10 years to Sep 30, 2026. It is also concentrated in tech and more volatile than the S&P 500.

QQQ or QQQM — which is better?

QQQM for buy-and-hold investors because it is cheaper (0.15% vs 0.18%). QQQ for traders who need its liquidity and options market.

Does QQQ pay dividends?

Yes, quarterly, but the yield is small — about 0.4% (30-day SEC yield, Oct 7, 2026).

What is TQQQ?

A ProShares fund that aims for 3× the Nasdaq-100's daily return. It resets daily and can lose much more than 3× over longer periods.

Is QQQ better than VOO?

QQQ has returned more over the last decade but holds only 100 stocks, mostly tech. VOO holds 500 companies across all sectors and costs 0.03% a year.

Is there a UCITS version of QQQ?

Yes. Invesco EQQQ and iShares CNDX both track the Nasdaq-100 and are the closest UK and EU equivalents, though their 0.30% fee is higher than QQQ's.
